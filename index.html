        How it works

        1. Tell it what you have. Type a list any way it comes out ("turkey edamame heavy cream tofu" works), or snap a photo of your fridge or pantry.
        2. See what you can make. Recipes are ranked by how many of your ingredients they use and how few you'd need to buy. The ones you can make right now glow.
        3. We assume you have the basics: salt, pepper, oil, butter, flour, sugar, and water. They're marked • in each recipe, where you can count them as missing instead.
        4. Get what's missing. Each recipe lists what you'd need, with links to stores and a list you can copy.

        Recipes and photos come from TheMealDB. Subscribe to New York Times Cooking? Search NYT Cooking for your ingredients.

        Where your data goes

        • Photos are shrunk in your browser and sent once to an AI service to spot ingredients. They're never stored, and only go to providers that don't keep or train on what they're sent.
        • Searches send only ingredient names to TheMealDB.
        • Your ingredient list and saved recipes stay in this browser. If you sign in to see your saves on other devices, they're stored with your email address.
        • Usage counts. We count visits, searches, and clicks to make Cook This better, without knowing who you are: no names, emails, or cookies.
        • Store links go straight to the store you pick.
